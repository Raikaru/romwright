## Recommendation

Order: **plumbing, then DWARF layouts, then inferred layouts (bottom-up only, gated), then donor SDK layouts; no solver upgrade before printing.**

1. **Plumbing first.** Nothing prints a struct until `answer_datatype` (provider.rs:1380) can encode a `<type metatype="struct">` with fields and the prototype text can name it; every layout source below needs that one path, and the print IR/printer already carry struct fields.
2. **DWARF layouts second.** Exact by construction (the compiler's own offsets), no inference risk, and the cheapest way to prove the plumbing: Capcom's debug build gives 4,460 scored truth struct-pointer parameters with exact layouts (5,221 functions carry debug info).
3. **Inferred layouts third, per function and bottom-up, not merged.** Isolated solves are 95.6% precise on offsets and 95.8% on widths (donor-truth games), and callee -> caller injection alone (`fwd`) adds 8.5 points of coverage at -0.6 points of precision with 93.7% of the added fields real. Gate the printing: 47.7% of truth `void *` and 56.4% of truth scalar-pointer parameters (24% on the DWARF games) also get a `Ty::Struct`, and a pointer used only at constant offsets cannot be told from a struct; the layout of one truth struct differs from function to function (mean Jaccard 0.33), so print per-function anonymous structs rather than a shared type, until a unification step exists. Two defects to fix with it: a loaded field is never given class `Ptr` (0% of accessed pointer-to-struct fields come out as pointers: 81% are `num`, 19% absent), and the 25% of those that the same body dereferences lose their pointee.
4. **Donor layouts last, and only the shared SDK part.** Types shared (same name and layout hash) by at least two independent donors are 24% of the struct types used by matched functions on N64, 12% on PSX, 8% on GC and on Wii (31% of the types that have a layout), 4% on PS2, 2% on GBA, 1% on NDS; weighted by the matched functions that use them: 14% N64, 6% PSX, 5% GC, 3% Wii, 3% GBA, 2% PS2, 1% NDS. Game-specific structs do not transfer; ship the shared set (libultra, PsyQ, Dolphin SDK, `sce*`) and keep the rest per game.

**Solver upgrade (algebraic subtyping): not before printing.** (a) Nesting: 15-66% of the truth structs contain a pointer to a struct, 25-88% of their pointer fields do, 0-16% are recursive; a recovered nested pointer today is a `num` field, and a pointee needs a global type-variable graph (recursive types), not a per-function `Field`. (b) Polymorphism: 7.0% of callee parameter slots that receive truth structs are reached with different structs (25.7% of those call sites, since the shared helpers are the hot ones). The damage is done by the caller -> callee joins, not by a missing scheme: with them (`whatif`) precision falls from 95.6% to 46.2% (donor games; 67.8% on DWARF) for +1.8 points of coverage over `fwd`, and 65.9% of the fields that merging adds at polymorphic slots are wrong for some call site. `shipped` is immune only because the decompiler's FuncProto overrides the inference, which is also why it propagates no structure at all (it equals `iso` to 0.1 point). Dropping the joins removes the harm; per-call-site schemes would recover at most the +1.8 points and the identity of types across functions, which is a separate (unification) problem.

## Limits

- Truth is the donor repository's source declarations for the five donor games (Persona 4, Fatal Frame, God Hand, Ratchet & Clank, Digital Devil Saga) and the compiler's debug info for Capcom Fighting Evolution (MWCC DWARF 1, `benchmarks/dwarf1_types.py` reader) and the Dungeon game image (ee-gcc DWARF 2, 10 struct-pointer parameters only). No GameCube/MWCC image with debug info exists in the corpus (the GC donors are decomp-symbols maps), so there is no GC row in Q1; GC and Wii are covered by Q2 from headers.
- Persona 4's repo is partly m2c-shaped, so its 'non-struct pointer' truth (`s32 *`, `u8 *`) is a guess in places: its false-struct rates are inflated; Fatal Frame, Ratchet & Clank, Digital Devil Saga and the DWARF games are hand-written C or compiler truth.
- Donor layouts are computed by this script with the PS2 ABI (`long` 8, pointers 4; `long` = 8 confirmed on the ee-gcc DWARF 2 image; Capcom's DWARF 1 measures 8 for MWCC). Only the MWCC side is checked against a compiler: 113 structs / 637 members of 14 Persona 4 units asserted with `sizeof` / member-address checks through the repo's own compile command (canary assertion proves the compiler reports failures): 1 struct disagrees. ee-gcc and SN ProDG layouts are not compiler-checked (the i386 ee-gcc cannot run on the hosts). Bit-field packing is approximate. C++ donors (Dark Cloud, Dark Chronicle) are not in Q1; classes with bases or virtuals have no layout in Q2.
- Only parameters with no floating-point parameter before them are scored (the integer slot is then unambiguous); structs passed by value, variadic functions and names with several differing source signatures are dropped.
- 'Accessed fields' are the members the body reaches through the parameter or a local alias (typed with this script's own member-access typer); accesses through a callee are not counted, so recall vs accessed is a small upper bound. Recall vs all fields is bounded by use and is low by construction.
- `shipped` decompiles every entry for Fatal Frame, Ratchet & Clank and the Dungeon image, and only a subset for the rest (Persona 4 355, Digital Devil Saga 1,111, God Hand 350, Capcom 350 functions, struct-pointer ones): the other functions then have no callee signature. The decompiler's signatures carry no structure, so this changes only scalar caps; the three full runs and the subset runs agree with `iso` to 0.1 point.
- Polymorphic callees are found from call-site argument types in the donor source (casts to `void *` looked through); callees without donor source (memcpy, memset) assume the i-th argument is integer slot i for i <= 3. Counts are lower bounds.
- Q3: the 20 are drawn (seed 20261012) from the 200-function m22-002 sample; only 24 of the 200 have member access, parse and compile as the unit's preprocessed text with the control exact against retail through this harness; the whole eligible pool is reported too. Typedef names are kept in the cast types; a member declared `volatile`/`const` would lose its qualifier (none of the 24 does).
